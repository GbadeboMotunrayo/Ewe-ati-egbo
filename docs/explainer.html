The problem

Buying African herbs in the UK is broken

The demand is not in question. The experience is.

Today a customer who wants bitter leaf, prekese, moringa or black soap hunts across a dozen disconnected places — and takes on all the risk themselves:

  • No trust. Who is this seller? Is the product what they say? If it never arrives, who do you call?
  • No information. Wrong names, no ingredients, no origin, no storage or safety guidance.
  • No compliance. Some products are food, some are cosmetics, some are medicines — and UK law treats each differently. Most informal sellers don't know, and don't care, until it becomes a problem.
  • Broken logistics. The genuinely hard part is moving product from Africa to the UK reliably, legally and affordably. This is where the pain — and the money — actually lives.
Everyone who has tried this built a prettier storefront. The storefront is the easy 20%. The unsolved 80% is the rail.

That gap is the opportunity. Whoever owns the trusted rail — the freight, the compliance, the verified supply — owns the category.

Compliance = de-risking

The part that scares everyone else is our feature

Herbal products in the UK are not one legal category. Get it wrong and you're an illegal pharmacy. We built the rules into the software so a seller can't get it wrong.

Every product is one of four classes

ClassExamplesRoute
A · Herb/botanicalBitter leaf, prekese, moringaFood / general law (FSA)
B · SupplementCapsules, powders, tincturesFood supplement (no disease claims)
C · CosmeticBlack soap, shea, oilsUK cosmetics (OPSS)
D · MedicineAnything sold “to treat”MHRA — closed by default

The claims engine (this is the magic)

When a seller writes a listing, our engine scans it. Say “cures malaria and treats fibroids” and it is blocked — instantly, with the reason. Ingredients run through a traffic-light list: 🟢 green — listable 🟠 amber — review 🔴 red — blocked

74 automated tests keep this honest — including a check that a seller can never quietly downgrade a risky herb to look harmless. Trust, provable in code.

Note: our classification is a design input; a UK-qualified regulatory/customs adviser signs off each class and corridor before launch. That review is scoped, not skipped.
