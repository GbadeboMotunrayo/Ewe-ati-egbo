# Ewe ati Egbo — The Pitch

A one-document case for why this is a business worth building and buying. Written for
an investor or acquirer, not a favour for a family member.

## One line

**We are building the trusted cross-border rail — commerce + logistics + compliance —
that moves African botanicals to the UK diaspora, and we own the freight network that
makes it work.**

## The problem

~2.5M+ people of African and Caribbean heritage live in the UK, and they buy
traditional herbs, botanicals and natural products constantly — for cooking, wellbeing
and culture. Today they buy through a broken patchwork: WhatsApp sellers, Instagram,
Etsy, suitcase importers, and a cousin who "knows someone." It is:

- **Untrusted** — no verified sellers, inconsistent quality, no buyer protection.
- **Unstructured** — wrong or missing names, no ingredient/origin info, no compliance.
- **Logistically painful** — the hard part is getting product *from Africa to the UK*
  reliably, legally and affordably. That is where the money and the pain both sit.

## The insight

Everyone who has tried this built a *storefront*. The storefront is the easy 20%. The
unsolved 80% is **cross-border logistics for a fragmented, low-trust supply chain** —
consolidating parcels from many small African sellers, clearing UK food/botanical
import rules, and delivering to the door at a price that works. Whoever owns that rail
owns the category.

## The solution

A four-sided platform:
- **Sellers** (African producers + UK diaspora shops) list verified, compliant products.
- **Customers** (UK) discover in their own vernacular ("ewuro" = "bitter leaf") and buy.
- **Cargo agents** (our own arm + onboarded third parties) carry the Africa→UK legs.
- **Us** — we verify, enforce compliance, route the freight, split the money (Paystack),
  and take a cut of both the product sale and the shipping.

## Why it's defensible (the moat)

| Layer | How hard to copy |
|---|---|
| **Logistics network** | Very hard. Onboarded cargo agents, proven corridors, customs know-how, consolidation. Compounds with volume; expensive and slow to rebuild. |
| **Compliance rail** | Hard. Product-class rules + UK import/food-safety handling done *for* the seller — a barrier that keeps informal competitors informal. |
| **Multilingual botanical data** | Medium. Vernacular↔botanical mapping across Yoruba/Igbo/Twi/etc. is a proprietary dataset and an SEO moat. |
| **Trust & verified supply** | Medium. Two-sided liquidity + verified sellers is a cold-start advantage once seeded. |

A storefront clone has none of these. That's the point.

## Business model (multiple takes on every order)

1. **Product commission** — % of each sale (indicative 8–12%).
2. **Logistics margin** — the fat line. Markup on freight; spread between agent rate
   and customer price; consolidation efficiency.
3. **Cargo-agent take** — platform fee on legs routed to third-party agents.
4. **Seller subscriptions / featured listings** (later).
5. **Data & wholesale** (later) — aggregated demand data, B2B bulk corridors.

Two revenue lines per transaction (product + shipping) is what makes the unit
economics fundable — logistics is not a cost centre we tolerate, it's a profit centre
we own.

## Market & expansion

Beachhead: **West African + Caribbean UK diaspora**. Then more African corridors
(Ghana, Kenya, South Africa origins), more UK demand, then replicate the rail into
**US and EU diaspora corridors** — same engine, new lanes. The logistics network and
compliance rail are the reusable assets.

## Why it's sellable / the exit thesis

An acquirer is buying **infrastructure, not an app**: a working cross-border freight
network, a compliant import pipeline, two-sided liquidity, and a proprietary botanical
dataset in a niche the big players ignore. Natural buyers: diaspora-focused
fintech/logistics players (the Africa→UK remittance/cargo world), a larger ethnic-foods
distributor, or a marketplace consolidator. The moat is operational, which is exactly
what strategic buyers pay premiums for.

## What we need to prove (de-risking order)

1. **Demand** — UK customers will buy from a new trusted brand. (cheap to test)
2. **Supply** — African/diaspora sellers will list and keep stock accurate.
3. **The rail works** — we can actually move a consolidated consignment Africa→UK,
   clear it, and deliver at a viable cost. **This is the make-or-break.**
4. **Unit economics** — product + logistics margin covers CAC with room to spare.

## The ask (fill in for a real raise)

Capital to (a) stand up the MVP marketplace + logistics ops, (b) seed the first
corridor with sellers and 1–2 cargo agents, (c) run 100 real end-to-end orders and
prove #3 above. Everything in `project-roadmap.md` sequences around proving the rail.
